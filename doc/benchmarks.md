# Benchmark instances

The benchmark instances of the literature are in the `benchmarks` archive of the data (see `data/data.json`), downloaded with:
```shell
python3 scripts/download_data.py --archives benchmarks
```

| Dataset | Problem | Instances | Format | Reference values |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [`queiroga2020`](#queiroga2020) | $1 \mid \text{p-batch}, B, \text{size}\_j, r_j \mid \sum w_j T_j$ | 810 (1,364 files) | `queiroga2020` | `data/data_single_twt.csv` |
| [`alfieri2021`](#alfieri2021) | $1 \mid \text{p-batch}, B, \text{size}\_j \mid \sum C_j$ | 320 | `alfieri2021` | `data/data_single_tct.csv` (no values yet) |
| [`chen2011`](#chen2011) | $1 \mid \text{p-batch}, B, \text{size}\_j \mid C_{\max}$ | 4,200 | XML (no reader yet) | none |

In all of them, the processing time of a batch is the largest processing time of its jobs, the total size of the jobs of a batch must not exceed the capacity $B$ of the machine, and the sizes, processing times, weights, release dates and due dates are integers.

The `data/data_<problem>.csv` files list the instances used for the comparisons, with their path relative to `data/`, their format, and the best known solution value and bound when they are known.

The instances of the unit tests are generated by this repository, see [`tests.md`](tests.md#test-instances).

## queiroga2020

E. Queiroga, R. G. S. Pinheiro, Q. Christ, A. Subramanian and A. A. Pessoa (2020). Iterated local search for single machine total weighted tardiness batch scheduling. *Journal of Heuristics*. [doi:10.1007/s10732-020-09461-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10732-020-09461-x)

**Problem.** $1 \mid \text{p-batch}, B, \text{size}\_j, r_j \mid \sum w_j T_j$: single machine, non-identical job sizes, release dates, total weighted tardiness. The instances can be used for the other objectives of the repository by changing the objective, since they have release dates, due dates and weights.

**Origin.** The instances used before for this problem were no longer available, so the authors generated a new benchmark. They started from the generation procedures of Chou and Wang (2008) and Mathirajan et al. (2010), and changed them because, in preliminary experiments, the resulting instances were often easy: the due dates were often so large that small instances had optimal solutions of value 0, and the release dates became irrelevant on large instances. The instances were published at `http://professor.ic.ufal.br/rian/Instances/TWTBSP.rar`.

**Generation** (Tables 3 and 4 of the paper), with $U(a, b)$ the discrete uniform distribution on $[a, b]$:
* number of jobs $n \in \{10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 50, 75, 100\}$;
* capacity $Q \in \{20, 40\}$;
* processing times $p_j \sim U(1, 30)$;
* sizes $s_j$: $U(1, 10)$, $U(5, 15)$ or $U(1, 20)$ for $Q = 20$, and $U(1, 20)$, $U(10, 30)$ or $U(1, 40)$ for $Q = 40$;
* release dates $r_j \sim U(0, R_2 \cdot C^*_{\max})$, with $R_2 \in \{0.1, 0.4, 0.8\}$ and $C^*_{\max}$ the expected makespan;
* due dates $d_j = r_j + p_j + U(1, 20)$;
* weights $w_j \sim U(1, 9)$.

The 3 size distributions and the 3 values of $R_2$ give 9 groups for each $n$ and $Q$, and the paper uses 5 instances per group: $9 \times 9 \times 2 \times 5 = 810$ instances.

**Files.** `queiroga2020/Capacity<Q>/<n>/<n>J_s<a>_r<b>/<i>.dat`, where `s<a>` is the upper bound of the size distribution (`s10`, `s15`, `s20` for $Q = 20$; `s20`, `s30`, `s40` for $Q = 40$) and `r<b>` is $100 R_2$ (`r10`, `r40`, `r80`). The archive contains more files than the paper uses:
* the directories `Capacity20/*/*` have 10 instances per group (20 for `Capacity20/20/20J_s15_r40`), and `Capacity40/50/50J_s30_r40` has 9;
* there are instances with 150 jobs (`Capacity20/150` and `Capacity40/150`), not used in the paper;
* some directories contain result files of the authors (`VND.txt`, `optimal solutions`), which are not instances.

`data/data_single_twt.csv` lists the 810 instances of the paper (instances 1 to 5 of each group, up to 100 jobs).

**Format** (`--format queiroga2020`): the first line gives the number of jobs and the capacity, then each line gives, for a job, its processing time, due date, size, weight and release date.

**Reference values.** `data/data_single_twt.csv` gives the best known solution value and bound of each of the 810 instances. On 330 of them the two are equal, so the optimal value is known: all the instances with 10 and 15 jobs, most with 20 jobs, and few above (none with 75 and 100 jobs). The local search found a solution below the best known value on `Capacity20/100/100J_s10_r10/1.dat` (35898 against 35930), see [`local_search.md`](local_search.md).

**Used by:** [`local_search.md`](local_search.md), [`tree_search.md`](tree_search.md).

## alfieri2021

A. Alfieri, A. Druetto, A. Grosso and F. Salassa (2021). Column generation for minimizing total completion time in a parallel-batching environment. *Journal of Scheduling*. [doi:10.1007/s10951-021-00703-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10951-021-00703-9)

**Problem.** $1 \mid \text{p-batch}, B, \text{size}\_j \mid \sum C_j$: single machine, non-identical job sizes, total completion time. The paper also solves the same job data on $m \in \{2, 3, 5\}$ identical parallel machines ($P_m \mid \text{p-batch}, B, \text{size}\_j \mid \sum C_j$); the files only describe one machine.

**Origin.** The generation of Uzsoy (1994) and Rafiee Parsa et al. (2016), the standard for this family of problems. The test instances of these two papers were not available, so the authors generated new ones of the same type. The source code and the instances were shared on a Google Drive folder given in the paper.

**Generation:**
* processing times $p_j \sim U(1, 100)$;
* sizes $s_j$ from one of 4 distributions: $\sigma_1$: $U(1, 10)$, $\sigma_2$: $U(2, 8)$, $\sigma_3$: $U(3, 10)$, $\sigma_4$: $U(1, 5)$;
* capacity $b = 10$; the paper also generated instances with $b = 30$ and $b = 50$, which are not in the archive;
* 10 instances for each number of jobs and size distribution.

The paper reports results for $n \in \{20, 40, 60, 80, 100\}$; the archive also contains $n \in \{10, 30, 50\}$: $8 \times 4 \times 10 = 320$ instances. The $\sigma_4$ instances (small jobs, many jobs per batch) are the hardest for column generation, because more columns are generated.

**Files.** `alfieri2021/inst_<n>_<σ>_<i>.dat`, with $\sigma \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ and $i \in \{1, \dots, 10\}$.

**Format** (`--format alfieri2021`): the first line gives the number of jobs and the capacity, then each line gives, for a job, its processing time and size. The weights are 1, and the objective is set to the total flow time (equal to the total completion time without release dates).

**Reference values.** The paper gives gaps and times per group of 10 instances, not the values per instance, so `data/data_single_tct.csv` lists the 320 instances without reference values.

Druetto and Grosso (2022), for the weighted version $1 \mid \text{p-batch}, B, \text{size}\_j \mid \sum w_j C_j$, use the same generation with weights $w_j \sim U(1, 50)$, and $s_j \sim U(1, 50)$ with $b = 50$ for an additional class; their instances are not in the archive.

## chen2011

H. Chen, B. Du and G. Q. Huang (2011). Scheduling a batch processing machine with non-identical job sizes: a clustering perspective. *International Journal of Production Research*, 49(19), 5755–5778. [doi:10.1080/00207543.2010.512620](https://doi.org/10.1080/00207543.2010.512620)

**Problem.** $1 \mid \text{p-batch}, B, \text{size}\_j \mid C_{\max}$: single machine, non-identical job sizes, makespan. They are the benchmark of the arc-flow MILP of Trindade et al. (2021) (`milp-arc-flow`), and a common benchmark for this problem.

**Generation** (scheme of Uzsoy, 1994), as observed in the files:
* number of jobs $n \in \{10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 300, 500\}$;
* capacity $B = 10$;
* processing times: `p1`: $U(1, 10)$, `p2`: $U(1, 20)$;
* sizes: `s1`: $U(1, 10)$, `s2`: $U(2, 4)$, `s3`: $U(4, 8)$;
* 100 instances for each number of jobs and each of the 6 classes: $7 \times 6 \times 100 = 4,200$ instances.

The release dates, due dates and deadlines are 0, and the weights are 1.

**Files.** `chen2011/<n> Jobs/p<a>s<b>/<i>.xml`, with `doi.url` pointing to the paper.

**Format.** XML (a .NET `DataSet`): `Job` elements with `ID`, `Size`, `ProcessingTime`, `ReleaseTime`, `Weight`, `DueDate` and `Deadline`, and `Machine` elements with `ID` and `Capacity`. The repository has no reader for this format yet (see [`roadmap.md`](roadmap.md)).

**Reference values.** None in the repository yet.
